A local survivor squad adds cooperation, orders and physical rescue to the six-trial prototype. One human survivor leads four AI allies against the AI Eye. Solo Survivor and human Eye remain selectable.

## Lead four physical allies

The squad contains one human survivor and four AI survivors. Their movement, carried objectives, trial interactions, exposure and damage occur in the shared arena. The AI Eye evaluates eligible survivors through its perception and legal targeting checks, including physical occlusion.

| Key | Order | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **B** | Work | Allies pursue the active trial objectives. |
| **F** | Follow | Allies follow the human leader. If the leader is inactive, this falls back to Work. |
| **V** | Hold | Allies hold their assigned positions. |

An order persists until changed. **Immediate evasion of a nearby visible shadow is the highest active-bot priority**: the ally cancels a rescue, takes a physically reachable safe step, then resumes its legitimate rescue or work. Otherwise, viable rescue takes priority and the escape phase overrides ordinary orders. These temporary tasks do not replace the stored order. The right-hand roster exposes each member's life state, health, current task, bleedout and rescue progress; Tab's map view hides the roster to preserve legibility.

## One rescue chance per member

A squad member's first lethal fall leaves them **downed for up to 35 seconds**. An active ally can rescue them by staying within **1.8 m**, maintaining clear physical line of sight, and holding **E for six continuous seconds**. AI rescuers use the same validated world conditions. Only one helper may rescue a subject at a time, and one helper cannot rescue two subjects concurrently.

Releasing the interaction, moving, leaving range, losing sight or taking a hit interrupts the rescue and resets its progress. Successful rescue restores **35 health** and gives **five seconds of protection**. The member's next lethal fall eliminates them; healing does not replenish their rescue allowance. Bleedout also eliminates a downed member. If nobody remains active to help, the remaining downed members are eliminated and the squad loses.

Pausing explicitly cancels ongoing rescue reservations and clears their progress, then freezes the countdowns. Resuming requires a fresh continuous hold; paused time cannot finish a rescue or consume the downed member's remaining bleedout.

An eliminated human can watch the surviving allies: **1–4 or the mouse wheel** cycles their actual cameras. The squad continues while eligible teammates remain. The existing shared escape outcome applies to the team.

Keeper's **Mend** restores **30 health** to the most injured eligible active team member, **including Keeper**. Allies qualify within **4 m** and clear physical line of sight. Keeper wins an equal-health tie; other ties retain stable actor order. This allows a critically injured Keeper to heal themself even when a less injured ally is nearby. The cooldown stays **35 seconds**. Mend cannot revive a downed member or undo elimination.

## Controls retained

Movement uses WASD / the corresponding physical ZQSD positions on AZERTY, or the arrow keys. Mouse looks, Shift sprints, Ctrl/C crouches, Space jumps, E interacts and Q uses the selected archetype ability. G returns carried cargo. Clock controls remain E forward, R reverse, X reset at the central station, and E on the central press to confirm. Eye controls remain mouse aim, 1–4 or wheel for fixed sockets, left click for traps, right click for mark and Q for summon. Escape pauses or closes the relevant panel.

The original solo recovery behavior remains separate from the squad's downed/rescue system. Choosing the new mode does not create an online party, a local second human controller, a network lobby or a reconnect service.

The local last-match report advances to **schema 3**. It records the requested `session_mode` independently from internal pawn/controller reuse, along with squad downs, revives, eliminations and orders. These are post-match records; no mid-match save or restore is supplied.

Any Godot error diagnostic fails the gate even with an exit code of zero. The packager rejects stale Windows file/product versions, verifies the PE32+ AMD64 header and ZIP CRCs, restores the export preset, and records the clean source revision, resource versions and executable hash.

The exact candidate archive then transfers to a **native Windows runner**. Its actual exported executable must pass nine headless startup cases: Survivor, Eye and Squad, each under First, Second and Seeded Mix. The readiness marker must identify both the requested role and the scene's actual `session_mode`. An accidental solo scene when requesting squad therefore fails the gate. Each case requests 240 frames, requires exit code zero, and rejects engine diagnostics.

Only a successful Windows job permits publication. The publishing job independently checks the archive SHA-256, exact source revision, all nine distinct case pairs, their actual session-mode fields and successful results. `WINDOWS_SMOKE.json` schema 2 records this evidence and is published beside the ZIP and `SHA256SUMS.txt`.

These Windows checks establish native headless startup. They do not establish interactive graphical Windows playthroughs, graphics-driver compatibility or hardware frame rates. Gameplay assertion counts, route observations and captured frames should be reported from the actual v0.3 logs, rather than copied from v0.2.

## Preserve earlier deliveries and backups

This workflow targets **v0.3.0**. It leaves v0.1.0 and v0.2.0 releases and tags intact, and preserves any already-existing v0.3.0 release or tag instead of overwriting it.
